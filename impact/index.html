Impact

Zuzana Irsova is Professor of Economics at Charles University in Prague, Distinguished Senior Lecturer at Anglo-American University and a Research Affiliate at METRICS, Stanford. She specializes in labor economics, international economics and meta-research. Her work brings together estimates from many studies to examine publication bias and the reliability of empirical evidence. Born in Roznava, Slovakia, she lives in Litomysl, Bohemia.

Who uses our research

Our results on intertemporal substitution are used in Thomas Sargent and John Stachurski’s Dynamic Programming and in work by Simon Johnson; Joseph Stiglitz cites our work on capital-labor substitution.

Our work on foreign direct investment is used in publications of the World Bank, IMF, OECD, UNCTAD, WTO and the Inter-American Development Bank.

Our work on daylight saving time is cited by the US Congressional Research Service, the European Parliamentary Research Service and the UK House of Commons Library.

Our work on gasoline demand, trade, the social cost of carbon and water demand is cited in Science, PNAS and Nature Water.

Others build on our data: David Slichter and Nhan Tran use eleven of our datasets in the Journal of Political Economy Microeconomics, and Christopher Carroll and coauthors open their paper in the American Economic Journal: Macroeconomics with our figure on habit formation.
